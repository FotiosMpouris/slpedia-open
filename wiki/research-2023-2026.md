## Papers & dated research outputs

### AAC for autistic people still mostly request-training (Mifsud et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/1460-6984.70251
- **People / org:** Selena Mifsud, Donna Thomas, Ruth Bowron, Rebecca Sutherland
- **Region:** Australia; International Journal of Language & Communication Disorders
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Systematic review: experimental AAC studies with autistic people still concentrate on children, aided systems, behavioural teaching, and simple requesting, with little on adults, generalisation, or quality-of-life outcomes. Clinically useful reality check when writing goals beyond “request preferred item.”

### AAC function patterns after caregiver training (Jiang & Wang, 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2025.105188
- **People / org:** Yuhan Jiang, Ting Wang
- **Region:** China; Research in Developmental Disabilities
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Corpus analysis of minimally verbal autistic children after caregiver-mediated low-tech AAC: use shifted from prompt-dependent requesting toward spontaneous engagement, joint attention, and environmental commentary, while regulatory and emotional functions stayed thin. Evidence that home AAC training can broaden functions, not only mands.

### AAC narrative intervention for children with autism (Almubark, Spencer & Foster, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2024.2433959
- **People / org:** Norah M. Almubark, Trina D. Spencer, Matthew E. Foster
- **Region:** United States / Saudi Arabia; Augmentative and Alternative Communication
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Story Champs–style AAC narrative intervention for three autistic children (ages 6–9): customized least-to-most prompting on story grammar and symbols improved retell complexity, symbol variety, generalization, and 3-week maintenance. Moves AAC goals past requesting into storytelling.

### AAC supports in a community museum for young children (Yacucci & Ballard, 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2025.2604825
- **People / org:** Amy L. Yacucci, Sarah L. Ballard
- **Region:** United States; Augmentative and Alternative Communication
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids, Broader SLP
- **Summary:** Utilization-focused evaluation of multimodal AAC supports in a children’s museum (majority of represented children autistic). Found gains in communication access and social awareness, plus a clear need for ongoing staff training, customization, and multiple accessible formats: AAC beyond the therapy room.

### AAC use in South African classrooms: challenges and implications (Terblanche, Pascoe & Harty, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/02656590241311063
- **People / org:** Camryn Terblanche, Michelle Pascoe, Michal Harty
- **Region:** South Africa; Child Language Teaching and Therapy
- **Desk filters:** Research, Broader SLP
- **Summary:** Exploratory focus-group study of AAC in South African classrooms: affordability, device security/crime risk, training gaps, multilingual realities, and implementation load shape whether AAC actually gets used. Essential Global South framing for feature-match thinking that assumes US/UK infrastructure.

### AAC visual aids for autistic youth in brain-stimulation procedures (Passarini et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1764016
- **People / org:** Sara Passarini, Fabio Quarin, Giulia Lazzaro, Floriana Costanzo, Andrea Battisti et al.
- **Region:** Italy (Bambino Gesù); Frontiers in Psychiatry
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Narrative review plus proposed AAC pictogram/visual-aid protocol so autistic youth can understand, consent to, and report sensations during non-invasive brain-stimulation (e.g., tDCS) procedures. Practical inclusion toolkit from an Italian pediatric research hospital, protocol-level, not yet a large clinical trial.

### Automating augmented input from partner speech (Holyfield et al., AJSLP 2024)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1044/2023_AJSLP-23-00224
- **People / org:** Christine Holyfield, Stephen MacNeil, Nicolette Caldwell, Tara O’Neill Zimmerman, Elizabeth Lorah et al.
- **Region:** United States; American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Wizard-of-Oz prototype that auto-pairs color photos with partner speech for minimally verbal autistic children. Versus a standard topic grid, the prototype raised visual attention and linguistic participation, a concrete path for AI-assisted aided input without waiting years for full product builds.

### COMPA: conversation context for common ground in AAC (Valencia et al., CHI 2024)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642762
- **People / org:** Stephanie Valencia, Jessica Huynh, Emma Y. Jiang, Yufei Wu, Teresa Wan et al.
- **Region:** United States; CHI 2024
- **Desk filters:** Research
- **Summary:** Browser extension for online group chat: live transcription, context marking, intent tags, and AI starter phrases so slower AAC typing does not strand speakers mid-thread. Triadic conversation study found better conversational flow and interest in real use.

### Context-aware AAC for academic communication (Griffen et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/01626434261420393
- **People / org:** Brenna Griffen, Sheida K. Raley, Christine Holyfield, Stephen MacNeil, Elizabeth R. Lorah, Jenna Burns
- **Region:** United States; Journal of Special Education Technology
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** ABAB study with three autistic preschoolers: a context-aware AAC prototype that surfaces partner-spoken response options beat a traditional LAMP-style topic grid on frequency, accuracy, and speed of academic answers (e.g., color knowledge). Early evidence that AI-assisted just-in-time options can unlock classroom participation.

### External challenges for AAC language learning (Holyfield et al., 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2025.2508485
- **People / org:** Christine Holyfield, Janice Light, Dana Nieder, Jamie Preece
- **Region:** United States; Augmentative and Alternative Communication
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids, Broader SLP
- **Summary:** Integrates lived experience with research on barriers that block language growth for people who need AAC: delayed access, undertrained clinicians, poor system fit, thin social opportunities, and unprepared partners. Clear agenda for service design, tech, and policy, with AAC users as equitable partners.

### Future technologies in AAC: scoping review (Benevento, Ciulla & Merlo, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2025.1607531
- **People / org:** Alessandra Desireè Benevento, Giuseppe Ciulla, Gianluca Merlo
- **Region:** Italy; Frontiers in Communication
- **Desk filters:** Research
- **Summary:** Frontiers scoping review of emerging AAC tech: AI and mobile apps dominate (input recognition, personalized generation, prediction, caregiver support, multilingual tools). Autism appears among target populations, but rigorous real-world evidence is still thin: a map of what is hyped vs. proven.

### NDBI + aided AAC meta-analysis for minimally speaking autistic children (Pope, Light & Laubscher, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-024-06382-7
- **People / org:** Lauramarie Pope, Janice Light, Emily Laubscher
- **Region:** United States; Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of naturalistic developmental behavioral interventions with and without aided AAC for autistic children with minimal speech. Language outcomes look stronger when AAC is in the package, though the AAC moderator itself was not statistically significant, useful evidence map for combining NDBI with SGDs.

### Nothing about AAC users without AAC users (Blasko, Light, McNaughton, Williams & Zimmerman, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2025.2514748
- **People / org:** Grant Blasko, Janice Light, David McNaughton, Bob Williams, Jordyn Zimmerman
- **Region:** United States; Augmentative and Alternative Communication
- **Desk filters:** Research, People, Broader SLP
- **Summary:** Lead paper from the Future of AAC Research special issue: argues AAC users must lead and co-create research, tech, and training priorities, not be token add-ons. Names risks (tokenism, ableism) and the practical shift toward mutual-respect collaboration under “nothing about us without us.”

### PaiCoach: AI-supported parent coaching for autistic children (Akemoğlu et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-026-07519-6
- **People / org:** Yusuf Akemoğlu, Zhaohui Li, Qingxiao Zheng, Emily Brooke Roberts, Samar Pratap Singh et al.
- **Region:** Turkey / international; Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids, People
- **Summary:** Single-case study of Parent’s AI Coach (PaiCoach): human-reviewed AI that scores parent–child shared-reading videos and returns expert-validated feedback on modeling, mand-model, and time-delay strategies. Early lane for AI parent coaching, complementary to (not a replacement for) AAC devices.

### Parent-reported AAC benefits and barriers in Nablus, Palestine (Jarrar et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-026-07161-2
- **People / org:** Hala Jarrar, Lana Malhis, Alaa Munawer, Moamen Omareya, Nagham Deek et al.
- **Region:** Palestine; BMC Pediatrics
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Cross-sectional survey of 75 caregivers of autistic children using AAC in Nablus: very high perceived benefits (more communication opportunities, better home interaction) alongside hard barriers: integrating AAC into routines and accessing programs. Rare Middle East field snapshot for global SLP practice.

### School professionals’ knowledge of autistic speech and AAC decisions (Zisk et al., 2024)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1793928
- **People / org:** Alyssa Hillary Zisk, Brandon Eddy, Amy L. Donaldson, Sara Cannalonga, Olivia Strickland et al.
- **Region:** United States; Seminars in Speech and Language
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids, People
- **Summary:** Participatory survey of 567 school-based professionals on autistic speech and AAC decision-making, co-led with autistic AAC users. Highlights knowledge gaps that drive under- or mis-prescription of AAC when speech is present but unreliable, and pushes for better training on intermittent/speech+AAC profiles.

### SGD speech output off + delayed reinforcement and verbal requests (Kaneda & Sasaki, 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-025-06910-z
- **People / org:** Moeka Kaneda, Ginga Sasaki
- **Region:** Japan; Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Alternating-treatment study with three Japanese children with autism and intellectual disability: turning off SGD synthetic speech and delaying reinforcement increased verbal requests while children kept using the device. Nuanced evidence that device settings can shape the balance of speech vs. AAC use.

### Teacher facilitators and barriers to AAC adoption in Saudi Arabia (Alhuzimi, 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2026.105227
- **People / org:** Talal E. Alhuzimi
- **Region:** Saudi Arabia; Research in Developmental Disabilities
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Survey of 101 Saudi teachers on AAC for autistic students: perceived usefulness and ease of use shape adoption via teacher attitudes. Practical takeaway: sustained hands-on training, equitable device access, and school-wide integration matter more than one-off workshops.

### XR-integrated BCI for AAC: systematic review (Zhang et al., 2026)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2026.1842938
- **People / org:** Peiran Zhang, Petra Karlsson, Darryl Chiu, Ran Zou, Cheng Zhang, Alistair McEwan, Bomin Sun, Haifeng Zhao
- **Region:** International; Frontiers in Human Neuroscience
- **Desk filters:** Research
- **Summary:** Systematic review of extended-reality + brain–computer interface AAC: only two eligible studies met criteria. Immersive P300 spelling can match conventional displays in demos, while eye-gaze often beats P300 in mixed reality; the field is still exploratory, not clinic-ready.

### “The less I type, the better”: LLMs for AAC users (Valencia et al., CHI 2023)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581560
- **People / org:** Stephanie Valencia, Richard Cave, Krystal Kallarackal, Katie Seaver, Michael Terry, Shaun K. Kane
- **Region:** United States / UK; CHI 2023
- **Desk filters:** Research
- **Summary:** CHI study with 12 adult AAC users on AI “Speech Macros” that expand brief input into fuller replies. Users saw real effort savings but insisted on control, personalization, privacy, and keeping their own voice, a design brief for any LLM-assisted AAC.

### SGD interventions for autistic preschoolers: systematic review (Therrien et al., 2025)

- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/07434618.2025.2479768 · [abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40164143)
- **People / org:** Therrien, Whalon, Nunes, Marti & Sellers
- **Region:** United States; *Augmentative and Alternative Communication*
- **Desk filters:** Research, Autism & kids
- **Summary:** Systematic review of 20 SGD intervention studies (1998–2023) for autistic preschoolers. Most targeted requesting; 11 showed moderate-to-strong effects; prompting was common, fewer than half met full ecological-validity criteria, and only seven measured social validity. Calls for work across diverse communicative functions.
